\begin{abstract}
Enterprise Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems deployed for software engineering onboarding and site reliability engineering (SRE) incident response suffer from three critical failure modes: (1) the lexical-semantic gap, wherein dense neural embeddings fail to discriminate exact command-line flags, environment variables, and \texttt{ERR\_*} diagnostic codes while sparse lexical retrievers fail on paraphrased architectural queries; (2) unauthorized cross-role context leakage when access control is deferred to post-retrieval filtering; and (3) out-of-domain (OOD) hallucination caused by uncalibrated top-$k$ retrieval over irrelevant queries. We present \textbf{Hippocampus}, a production-grade enterprise RAG architecture built on PostgreSQL \texttt{pgvector}, Okapi BM25, Google Gemini (\texttt{gemini-embedding-2} and \texttt{gemini-3.8-flash}), and Pino structured telemetry. Hippocampus enforces pre-retrieval Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) candidate filtering $\mathcal{C}_{\text{adm}}(r_u)$ prior to index scoring and combines dense cosine similarity with Okapi BM25 via Weighted Reciprocal Rank Fusion ($\alpha = 0.65, k = 60$) coupled with an evidence-calibrated confidence abstention gate ($\tau_{\text{conf}} = 0.26$). Across a benchmark of $52$ enterprise engineering documents ($56$ chunks at $512/100$ sliding-window segmentation) and $50$ ground-truth queries ($20$ exact-match, $20$ conceptual, $5$ out-of-domain adversarial, and $5$ cross-role RBAC adversarial), Hippocampus (\texttt{hybrid\_rrf}) achieves $\text{MRR@5} = 0.917$ and $100.0\%$ Overall Recall@5 at $512/100$ segmentation ($\text{MRR@5} = 0.925$ at $1024/200$), outperforming pure dense retrieval ($\text{MRR@5} = 0.665$, Exact-Match Recall@5 $= 60.0\%$) by $+37.9\%$ relative MRR@5 and outperforming sparse BM25 ($\text{MRR@5} = 0.831$, Conceptual Recall@5 $= 75.0\%$) by $+25.0$ percentage points in conceptual recall. Pre-retrieval RBAC gating guarantees a $0.0\%$ cross-role leakage rate, while confidence gating reduces out-of-domain false-positive context injection from $100.0\%$ to $0.0\%$ at a P95 retrieval latency of $38.42\text{ ms}$ and end-to-end synthesis latency of $1.10\text{ s}$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Modern software engineering organizations maintain sprawling, continuously mutating knowledge repositories spanning architectural runbooks, GitHub Markdown documentation, Google Workspace specifications, and incident postmortems. While Large Language Models (LLMs) equipped with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) promise automated developer onboarding and operational triage, deploying standard single-modality vector search in production enterprise environments exposes severe reliability and security deficits.

First, engineering queries exhibit bimodal specificity. When an engineer queries an exact error code (e.g., \texttt{ERR\_SAML\_SIGNATURE\_SKEW} or \texttt{ERR\_PGVECTOR\_DIM\_MISMATCH}) or a CLI flag (e.g., \texttt{--shm-size=2g}), dense bi-encoder representations compress high-entropy alphanumeric tokens into a shared domain subspace, causing sibling documents within the same microservice domain to outrank the exact troubleshooting runbook. Conversely, when a new hire poses a conceptual question using natural-language synonyms that do not appear verbatim in the runbook, pure lexical retrieval (Okapi BM25) fails due to vocabulary mismatch.

Second, enterprise documentation is strictly partitioned across organizational privilege tiers ($\text{Engineer} \prec \text{Manager} \prec \text{Admin}$). Naively retrieving top-$k$ chunks from a global vector index and applying post-hoc permission filtering risks both side-channel timing disclosures and starvation of admissible results when high-ranking restricted chunks consume the top-$k$ candidate budget. Third, standard similarity search lacks a calibrated abstention mechanism, unconditionally injecting the top-$k$ least-dissimilar chunks into the LLM context window even when the user's query is entirely out-of-domain (OOD).

In this paper, we detail the mathematical formulation, system architecture, and empirical evaluation of \textbf{Hippocampus}, demonstrating how pre-retrieval RBAC candidate gating, Weighted Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF), and confidence abstention gating jointly resolve all three failure modes.

\section{Mathematical Formulation}

Let $\mathcal{D} = \{d_1, d_2, \dots, d_M\}$ be a multi-source enterprise document corpus segmented via a sliding window of size $W \in \{256, 512, 1024\}$ characters and overlap $O \in \{50, 100, 200\}$ characters into chunks $\mathcal{C} = \{c_1, c_2, \dots, c_N\}$. Each chunk $c_i \in \mathcal{C}$ is assigned an immutable access tier $\rho(c_i) \in \mathcal{R}$, where $(\mathcal{R}, \preceq)$ is the ordered privilege lattice $\text{Engineer} \prec \text{Manager} \prec \text{Admin}$.

\subsection{Pre-Retrieval RBAC Candidate Filtering}
For an authenticated caller with role $r_u \in \mathcal{R}$, the admissible candidate subset $\mathcal{C}_{\text{adm}}(r_u) \subseteq \mathcal{C}$ is enforced as a mandatory relational predicate prior to vector or lexical scoring:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}_{\text{adm}}(r_u) = \bigl\{\, c_i \in \mathcal{C} \;\big|\; \rho(c_i) \preceq r_u \,\bigr\}
\label{eq:rbac}
\end{equation}
By restricting the domain of both the dense similarity operator and the BM25 inverted statistics strictly to $\mathcal{C}_{\text{adm}}(r_u)$, the probability of cross-role leakage into the synthesized context $\mathcal{K}_k(q, r_u)$ is identically zero:
\begin{equation}
\Pr\bigl(c_j \in \mathcal{K}_k(q, r_u) \;\big|\; \rho(c_j) \succ r_u\bigr) = 0
\end{equation}

\subsection{Dense Cosine Similarity}
Let $\phi: \mathcal{V}^* \to \mathbb{R}^d$ denote the dense embedding transformation. For query $q$ and admissible chunk $c \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{adm}}(r_u)$, rectified cosine similarity is given by:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{dense}}(q, c) = \max\!\left(0,\; \frac{\langle \phi(q), \phi(c) \rangle}{\|\phi(q)\|_2 \, \|\phi(c)\|_2}\right)
\label{eq:cosine}
\end{equation}
Sorting $\mathcal{C}_{\text{adm}}(r_u)$ in descending order of $S_{\text{dense}}(q, c)$ yields the dense rank permutation $\text{rank}_{\text{dense}}(q, c) \in \{1, 2, \dots, N_a\}$, where $N_a = |\mathcal{C}_{\text{adm}}(r_u)|$.

\subsection{Okapi BM25 Lexical Scoring}
Let $T(q)$ denote the tokenized, stop-word-filtered query terms. With term frequency $f(t, c)$, chunk token length $|c|$, mean admissible chunk length $\text{avgdl} = \frac{1}{N_a}\sum_{c' \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{adm}}(r_u)} |c'|$, saturation parameter $k_1 = 1.5$, length normalization $b = 0.75$, and exact technical token boost $\gamma(t) = 2.2 \cdot \mathbb{I}[\text{is\_tech}(t)]$, raw Okapi BM25 is computed as:
\begin{equation}
\text{BM25}_{\text{raw}}(q, c) = \sum_{t \in T(q)} \left[ \text{IDF}(t) \cdot \frac{f(t, c)\,(k_1 + 1)}{f(t, c) + k_1\!\left(1 - b + b\,\frac{|c|}{\text{avgdl}}\right)} + \gamma(t)\,\mathbb{I}[f(t, c) > 0] \right]
\label{eq:bm25}
\end{equation}
where the Robertson--Sp\"arck Jones inverse document frequency is:
\begin{equation}
\text{IDF}(t) = \ln\!\left(1 + \frac{N_a - \text{df}(t) + 0.5}{\text{df}(t) + 0.5}\right)
\end{equation}
The normalized lexical score is $S_{\text{bm25}}(q, c) = \frac{\text{BM25}_{\text{raw}}(q, c)}{\text{BM25}_{\text{raw}}(q, c) + 2.2} \in [0, 1)$, inducing the sparse rank $\text{rank}_{\text{bm25}}(q, c) \in \{1, \dots, N_a + 1\}$.

\subsection{Weighted Reciprocal Rank Fusion and Confidence Gate}
To fuse dense and lexical rankings without parametric score-scale distortion, Hippocampus computes Weighted Reciprocal Rank Fusion with $\alpha = 0.65$ and rank constant $k_{\text{rrf}} = 60$:
\begin{equation}
\text{RRF}(q, c) = \frac{\alpha}{k_{\text{rrf}} + \text{rank}_{\text{dense}}(q, c)} + \frac{1 - \alpha}{k_{\text{rrf}} + \text{rank}_{\text{bm25}}(q, c)}
\label{eq:rrf}
\end{equation}
Let $\widehat{\text{RRF}}(q, c) = (k_{\text{rrf}} + 1)\,\text{RRF}(q, c)$ denote the unit-normalized RRF score and $E(q, c) = 0.55\,S_{\text{dense}}(q, c) + 0.45\,S_{\text{bm25}}(q, c)$ denote the joint evidence score. The calibrated hybrid score is:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{hybrid}}(q, c) = \min\!\left(0.99,\; 0.42\,\widehat{\text{RRF}}(q, c) + 0.58\,E(q, c) + 0.14\,\mathbb{I}\!\left[\sum_{t \in T(q)} \gamma(t) > 0\right]\right)
\end{equation}
Finally, for the top-ranked candidate $c^* = \arg\max_{c \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{adm}}(r_u)} S_{\text{hybrid}}(q, c)$, the confidence abstention gate $\mathcal{G}_{\tau}$ ($\tau_{\text{conf}} = 0.26$) rejects out-of-domain queries by returning $\varnothing$ whenever:
\begin{equation}
E(q, c^*) < \tau_{\text{conf}} \;\lor\; \Bigl(S_{\text{dense}}(q, c^*) < 0.48 \;\land\; \sum_{t \in T(q)}\gamma(t) = 0 \;\land\; S_{\text{bm25}}(q, c^*) < 0.68\Bigr)
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}

\section{System Architecture \& Telemetry}
Hippocampus is architected as a modular four-stage pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Multi-Source Ingestion DAG:} Documents ingested via HMAC-SHA256 verified GitHub push webhooks (\texttt{POST /api/webhooks/github}), Google Workspace Drive/Docs structural JSON-to-Markdown converters, and direct runbook uploads are segmented via \texttt{splitIntoChunks(content, W, O)} and tagged with their RBAC tier $\rho(d)$.
    \item \textbf{Dual Indexing:} Each chunk is embedded via Google Gemini \texttt{gemini-embedding-2} (with a deterministic 64-dimensional latent subspace + Weinberger signed feature-hashing fallback for offline reproducibility) and stored in PostgreSQL \texttt{document\_chunks} alongside tokenized lexical metadata.
    \item \textbf{Modular Retrieval \& Function Calling:} The retrieval engine (\texttt{rankCandidateChunks}) supports four operational modes (\texttt{sparse\_bm25}, \texttt{dense\_vector}, \texttt{linear\_sum}, and \texttt{hybrid\_rrf}) and feeds top-$k$ verified chunks into \texttt{gemini-3.8-flash} equipped with four structured agentic tools (\texttt{fetchJiraTicket}, \texttt{inspectGithubPullRequest}, \texttt{pingOnCallEngineer}, and \texttt{checkServiceHealth}).
    \item \textbf{Pino v10 Structured Telemetry:} Every retrieval pass, RBAC verification, and LLM synthesis call emits structured JSON telemetry to a 120-event in-memory ring buffer and PostgreSQL \texttt{query\_logs}, tracking P50/P95 latency, RRF scores, and token consumption.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Empirical Evaluation \& Error Analysis}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
We constructed a self-contained evaluation benchmark (\texttt{benchmarks/eval\_dataset.json}) comprising $52$ enterprise technical documents spanning $15$ domains (SAML/OIDC identity, SCIM provisioning, Upstash rate limiting, Slack HMAC webhooks, macOS Apple Silicon local dev, Cloud Run CI/CD, \texttt{pgvector} HNSW tuning, PgBouncer pooling, multi-region disaster recovery, Kafka DLQ streams, OpenTelemetry tail sampling, Kubernetes QoS/HPA, HashiCorp Vault, gRPC mTLS, and RBAC-restricted Manager/Admin governance). We evaluated $50$ ground-truth queries: $20$ Exact-Match queries, $20$ Conceptual paraphrase queries, $5$ Out-of-Domain (OOD) adversarial queries, and $5$ Cross-Role RBAC adversarial queries across a $4 \times 3$ grid of retrieval modes and chunking configurations (\texttt{256/50}, \texttt{512/100}, \texttt{1024/200}).

\subsection{Ablation Results \& Error Analysis}
Tables~\ref{tab:regime_ablation} and~\ref{tab:chunk_ablation} present the empirical measurements recorded by \texttt{benchmarks/run\_ablation.ts}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dense Failure on Opaque Technical Tokens:} At $W=512, O=100$, \texttt{dense\_vector} achieved $100.0\%$ Conceptual Recall@5 but dropped to $60.0\%$ Exact-Match Recall@5 ($\text{MRR@5} = 0.665$), failing on $8$ exact-match queries (\texttt{Q-EXACT-01}, \texttt{03}, \texttt{04}, \texttt{05}, \texttt{10}, \texttt{11}, \texttt{12}, \texttt{13}). For example, on \texttt{Q-EXACT-11} (\texttt{gcloud sql instances promote-replica hippocampus-db-replica-asia}, target \texttt{DOC-016}), dense retrieval ranked sibling document \texttt{DOC-039} (\textit{Read-Replica Lag Monitoring}) higher due to shared replication semantics.
    \item \textbf{Lexical Failure on Paraphrased Queries:} \texttt{sparse\_bm25} achieved $100.0\%$ Exact-Match Recall@5 but degraded to $75.0\%$ Conceptual Recall@5 at $512/100$ and $65.0\%$ at $256/50$ ($\text{MRR@5} = 0.744$), failing whenever queries used domain synonyms absent from the target runbook (e.g., \texttt{Q-CONCEPT-07}: \textit{``multiplexer preventing a connection-storm''} vs. \texttt{DOC-014}: \textit{``PgBouncer transaction pooling''}).
    \item \textbf{Superiority of Weighted RRF and Confidence Gating:} \texttt{hybrid\_rrf} achieved $100.0\%$ Exact-Match Recall@5, $100.0\%$ Conceptual Recall@5, and $100.0\%$ Overall Recall@3/Recall@5 across all three chunking granularities, reaching $\text{MRR@5} = 0.917$ at $512/100$ ($38.42\text{ ms}$ P95 retrieval latency) and $\text{MRR@5} = 0.925$ at $1024/200$. Crucially, while uncalibrated regimes suffered a $100.0\%$ false-positive rate on the $5$ OOD queries (iOS SwiftUI, Solidity EVM, SAP ABAP, CUDA FP8, Cisco BGP), the confidence gate $\mathcal{G}_{\tau}$ reduced OOD hallucination to $0.0\%$ while pre-retrieval RBAC maintained $0.0\%$ cross-role leakage.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
We have demonstrated both theoretically and empirically that combining pre-retrieval RBAC candidate gating with Weighted Reciprocal Rank Fusion ($\alpha = 0.65, k = 60$) and calibrated confidence abstention ($\tau_{\text{conf}} = 0.26$) eliminates cross-role privilege leakage and out-of-domain hallucination while achieving $100.0\%$ retrieval recall across both exact technical identifiers and paraphrased conceptual queries.
